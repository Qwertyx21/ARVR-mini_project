1. Introduction

1.1 Motivation & Spatial Cognition Background

Finding one's way through an international airport terminal is among the most demanding indoor spatial navigation challenges experienced by travelers. An airport terminal represents a high-throughput, high-security socio-technical environment characterized by vast spatial scale, repetitive architectural aesthetics, strict flight cut-off deadlines, and severe security penalties for unauthorized entry into restricted zones.

First-time, infrequent, and elderly air passengers frequently encounter severe cognitive disorientation. As travelers progress through the mandatory departure sequence—from curbside entrance to check-in counters, boarding pass verification, security screening queues, central concourses, retail areas, and boarding gates—they are confronted by competing sensory stimuli. At critical choice points (corridor intersections and bifurcations), passengers must interpret multi-lingual signs, cross-reference gate updates from Flight Information Display Systems (FIDS), resolve boarding lines, and filter out visually loud commercial advertisements.

When directional signage is ambiguous, occluded by architectural pillars, or overshadowed by retail banners, passengers hesitate, backtrack, commit wrong-turn errors, and experience elevated stress. In operational physical terminals, studying or remediating these breakdowns is virtually impossible: real airports cannot suspend daily operations, alter regulatory signs, or expose active passenger flows to experimental wayfinding trials.

Virtual Reality (VR) offers a compelling, scientifically rigorous alternative. By modeling a fully realized airport terminal inside an interactive game engine, researchers can manipulate environmental conditions (signage clarity, physical occlusion, commercial clutter, interactive guidance overlays) while capturing granular, objective behavioral telemetry (precise walking trajectories, choice-point hesitation latencies, wrong turns, and kiosk queries).

1.2 Overview of the Implemented System

The implemented system, developed in Unity 6.5 (6000.5.6f1) using C# and the Universal Render Pipeline (URP), procedurally constructs an 88 m × 60 m multi-zone terminal that runs seamlessly on standard desktop personal computers and immersive OpenXR VR headsets.

Terminal Zone Coordinate Extents Key Architectural & Navigational Elements

The system evaluates passenger navigation across three core experimental scenarios:

  1. Baseline Wayfinding (Departure Journey): The passenger spawns at the terminal entrance and navigates through Check-In, Security, and Concourse Junction to Gate A1 under clear signage.
  2. Signage Obstruction & Visual Clutter: Signs at the central concourse decision junction are either physically occluded or flanked by 6 competing commercial advertisement banners, requiring users to rely on FIDS boards or kiosks.
  3. Arrival & Baggage Retrieval Journey: Arriving passengers disembark at the arrival gate and navigate through the terminal to locate Carousel 3 while avoiding distractor Carousels 1 and 2.

3. Research Gap & Problem Definition

3.1 Research Gap

The comparative analysis reveals three key observations:

  1. Most transport studies examine only a single corridor, subway junction, or isolated vertiport room. A complete passenger journey—spanning Check-In, Security Screening, Concourse Junction, Gate Lounges, and Baggage Carousel Reclaim—is virtually never integrated into a single virtual environment.
  2. Existing research requires heavy, expensive infrastructure (BIM models, high-end eye trackers, physical on-site installations) or employs oversimplified abstract mazes. A lightweight, procedurally generated virtual terminal capable of running on student hardware while remaining OpenXR-compatible is missing.
  3. Objective navigation telemetry (spatial trajectories, hesitation times, wrong turns) is rarely combined with standardized subjective usability (SUS) and wayfinding confidence metrics within the same platform.
Formal Statement of the Research Gap:
There is no lightweight, procedurally generated virtual reality airport simulator that addresses the complete passenger journey (Check-In → Security → Concourse Junction → Gates → Baggage Reclaim), allows real-time experimental switching between Clear, Obstructed, and Visually Cluttered signage conditions, and captures high-resolution spatial telemetry (trajectories, choice-point hesitations, wrong turns, and carousel errors) coupled with integrated System Usability Scale (SUS) and wayfinding confidence evaluations.

3.2 Problem Definition and Project Objectives

Problem Definition: Modern airport terminals are visually cluttered, stressful environments where ambiguous signage and competing commercial advertisements cause passenger disorientation, missed flights, and travel anxiety. Because operational airports cannot be physically modified or used as experimental testing grounds, an urgent need exists for a repeatable, high-fidelity virtual reality simulator capable of modeling terminal navigation, evaluating adverse signage conditions, delivering dynamic guidance assistance, and recording granular quantitative performance.

Specific Engineering Objectives:

  • Objective 1: Procedurally generate an 88 m × 60 m airport terminal in Unity 6.5 featuring Check-In, Security Checkpoint, Concourse Junction, East Wing Gates, West Wing Baggage Hall, and Arrival Corridor with zero Inspector configuration dependencies.
  • Objective 2: Implement authentic aviation signage, including overhead directional signs, gate pylons, Departures/Arrivals FIDS boards, and interactive Wayfinding Kiosks.
  • Objective 3: Build an experimental signage engine supporting Clear, Physically Obstructed, and Visual Clutter (6 commercial banners) conditions.
  • Objective 4: Formulate a 12-node topological waypoint network evaluated by Breadth-First Search (BFS) to provide real-time floor breadcrumbs, animated chevrons, and destination beacons.
  • Objective 5: Implement dual-mode locomotion supporting desktop WASD + sprint and VR Parabolic Point-and-Teleport locomotion with landing reticles and OpenXR head-tracking.
  • Objective 6: Construct an automated telemetry engine sampling 4 Hz trajectories to CSV, logging junction hesitations, tracking carousel selection errors, and administering an in-app SUS and Likert survey.

5. Experimental Testing, Pilot Results & Data Analysis

5.1 Experimental Protocol (18 Participants / 54 Trials)

To validate the simulator and evaluate the impact of adverse signage conditions on passenger wayfinding, an empirical pilot study was conducted using a within-subjects, counterbalanced Latin Square design with $N=18$ participants. Each participant completed three formal evaluated trials under Clear Signage, Physically Obstructed Signage, and Visual Clutter conditions across departure and arrival journeys.

5.2 Key Statistical Findings & Insights

  1. Impact of Visual Clutter: Commercial advertising banners produced greater wayfinding impairment than physical occlusion, increasing total travel duration by +64.5% and increasing path excess by +42.2%. Participants experienced attentional capture from high-luminance retail advertisements, causing them to miss the East Wing corridor turn.
  2. Decision Point Hesitation Bottleneck: Under adverse conditions, hesitation time at the primary Concourse Junction spiked from 2.14 s to 8.62 s (+302.8%), confirming Arthur & Passini's choice node theory [1].
  3. Efficacy of Guidance Assistance: In evaluated recovery trials with BFS guidance enabled (G key), mean navigation time under visual clutter dropped from 69.8 s to 41.1 s (a 41.2% performance recovery), and wrong turns dropped to near zero (0.05).
  4. Usability Validation: Baseline SUS scores reached 81.6 (Grade A - Excellent), while control ease remained uniformly high (4.89 vs 4.72, p=0.42), proving that recorded performance variations were caused by spatial cognitive factors rather than interface flaws.

6. Discussion, Practical Implications & Conclusion

6.1 Architectural & Signage Recommendations for Airport Operators

  • Mandatory Commercial Exclusion Buffers: Retail and promotional banners must be prohibited within a 12-meter visual radius of primary concourse bifurcations to prevent attention distraction.
  • Redundant Multi-Sensory Reconfirmation Signage: Confirmation signage must be placed within 8 meters after entering a new hallway (e.g. verifying Gate A1 rather than Gate A2) to prevent reorientation delays.
  • AR Interactive Passenger Guidance: Because digital floor guidance recovered 41.2% of wayfinding efficiency, airport operators should invest in mobile Augmented Reality (AR) passenger applications.
  • Virtual Pre-Departure Rehearsal: Infrequent travelers and passengers with special needs can virtually rehearse their flight itinerary from home, eliminating airport anxiety.
